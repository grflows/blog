The Umami of life

It waves up and down, washing away the banal moments of life into an ever-coalescing remembrance of the past.


The captain stares at the undulating blue floor his tiny vessel trudges. Nothing but blue around him, a tiny spec of land colors trapped between two multitudes; one an unforgiving azure, and the other a forgetful teal.


His eyes were red from the salty air. His beard is an unkempt silver spool of a nest. His hat had long stopped grieving over the gentle hand with a needle and a thread. His pipe an old friend with scars and cracks filled with the melancholy of unrest. His calloused fingers were marked by the darkened grease of manual labor and the doming nails of a chain smoker.


The captain was in his seventies, smelled of the sea, tobacco smoke, and engine oil. And like the frail body and black hair burned ashen white by time, his heart had long stopped racing. As if, by the cruelty of self-delusion, it had also believed that it had experienced all of life. From passion-dulling richness to stomach-piercing poverty. From lowering his sleeping child to their wooden cradle, to lowering his sleeping child into one dug in the dirt. Life wasn't holy or wholly traitorous to the captain. It just was. And so his heart believed that it had long delivered the expected, and spent the remaining decades in a slow rhythm, as if rationing whatever beats left in it.


Yet still, the old sailer took his steaming boat across the fog of dawn, and fished further than most. Although fishing was just an excuse for sailing, his net was sometimes plentiful. And such days brought a flavor to the captain's days. A flavor that age and the happenings of life could not make it dull or unwanted.

There's a flavor to living, something between a feeling and a desire. A subtle dimension to the human experience, perhaps closer to a serene state of mind. It can get familiar, just like joy and anger, but it never dulls down. And it's what the captain seeks it in the daily sail.


"It's like adding sea weed to my food," said the captain. "An Umami to life," he continued, "No matter how your life tastes, it can always use a bit of it.


"But it's elusive, you have to savor hard to get a taste. And once you do, you'll taste it every time."


"Search for it in the daily grind. Clear your mind and savor the moment, and you'll catch a taste of it. No matter how dull life becomes, it's a flavor you'll never get tired of."
